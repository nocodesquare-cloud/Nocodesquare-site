All three tools connect apps and run workflows. Once you understand one, the others come quickly, because triggers, actions and data mapping work much the same way. So the first choice matters less than people think. Cost is usually the deciding factor.

## How each one charges

* **Make** bills in credits, and most actions use one credit. Its free plan includes 1,000 credits a month.
* **n8n** cloud bills per workflow run, however many steps that run contains. On annual billing, its pricing page lists Starter at €20 a month for 2,500 runs and Pro at €50 a month for 10,000. There is also a self-hosted version available on GitHub, where you run it on your own server.
* **Zapier** bills per task, which roughly means each action that completes. It is known for being very easy to use and for connecting to a huge number of apps.

## The same job on each tool

Say your workflow has five steps and runs 1,000 times a month. On Make, that is about 5,000 credits. On Zapier, it is roughly 4,000 tasks, since the starting trigger does not count. On n8n, it is 1,000 runs.

Longer workflows make the gap wider. That is why n8n becomes attractive for complex systems, and why it is worth doing this sum before you promise a client a monthly cost.

## What we would tell a friend

* **Complete beginner:** start with Make. You can see the data moving through each step on a visual canvas, which makes learning much easier.
* **Comfortable with technical things, or building heavy workflows:** learn n8n. Self-hosting can keep costs down, but you become responsible for keeping the server running, updated and secure.
* **Connecting two popular apps quickly:** Zapier is often the fastest way to get it done.

## Before you commit

Pricing changes often, so check each tool's own pricing page rather than a blog post, including this one. Then work out the cost in naira or shillings for your real volume, and leave room for exchange-rate moves.
