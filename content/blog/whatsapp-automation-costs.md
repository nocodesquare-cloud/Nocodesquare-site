For many African businesses, WhatsApp is the main customer channel, so it is often the first thing clients ask you to automate. Pricing rules on the WhatsApp Business Platform changed recently, and an old tutorial may give you the wrong numbers.

## First, who pays

This pricing applies to businesses using the platform. Meta does not charge for messages that users send to businesses. A claim that ordinary Nigerians would pay ₦14 a message was fact-checked as misleading.

## How billing works

Since July 1, 2025, Meta has charged per delivered message, based on the message's category and the recipient's country. The main categories are:

- **Marketing.** Template messages such as promotions and offers.
- **Utility.** Template messages about something already happening, such as an order update or a payment reminder.
- **Authentication.** Template messages such as one-time passwords.
- **Service.** Ordinary free-form replies, sent after a customer messages you.

## What changed on October 1, 2026

- Each business phone number now gets **1,000 free service messages a month**. Charges begin with message 1,001.
- **Utility templates sent during an open customer-service window are now charged** at published rates. They used to be free.
- Businesses needed a payment method on file by September 30, 2026, or their service messages could stop being delivered.

Nigeria has its own line on Meta's rate card. Rates can change at the start of a quarter, so take the current figure from Meta directly and avoid quoting a number from a social media post.

## What to do as a builder

1. **Count messages by category.** "How many customers?" is the wrong question. Ask how many marketing, utility and service messages each one will receive.
2. **Audit old automations.** A flow that sends order updates inside the service window may have become a new cost since October.
3. **Send fewer, better messages.** Combine three status updates into one where you can.
4. **Build in consent and an exit.** Only message people who opted in, make it easy to stop, and hand off to a person when someone needs one.
5. **Put the cost in your proposal.** Make clear that WhatsApp charges are separate from your fee, so a client is not surprised later.
