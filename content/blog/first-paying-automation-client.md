Most people wait too long, because they think they need more courses first. What gets you hired is proof that you can solve one real problem.

## 1. Build one thing for a real person

Find a business you can already reach: a relative's shop, a friend's clinic or the logistics company a former classmate works at. Ask what they repeat every week and hate doing. Then build a fix. Doing this first one free or cheaply is fine, as long as you agree you can talk about the result.

## 2. Measure before and after

Note how long the task took before, and how long it takes now. "This used to take Amaka two hours a day and now takes ten minutes" will do more for you than a list of tools. Ask for permission to share it, and ask for a short testimonial while the win is fresh.

## 3. Sell the result, not the tools

A business owner does not wake up wanting an n8n workflow. They want fewer missed orders, faster follow-up or fewer hours of admin. Say "I'll make sure every lead gets a reply within five minutes", then explain the tools only if they ask.

## 4. Reach out with something specific

Generic messages get ignored. Look at how a business works, spot one thing you could improve, and say so: "I tried booking on your site and only got a form with no confirmation. I can fix that so customers get an instant reply." A short screen recording of a demo works even better.

## 5. Price it sensibly

- **Set a fixed project price.** Charging by the hour punishes you for getting faster.
- **Tie it to the value.** Work out roughly what the problem costs them each month, and price well below that.
- **Take part of it up front.** A deposit filters out people who are not serious.
- **Charge for upkeep.** Tools change and things break. A monthly maintenance fee is fair, and it gives you steadier income.
- **Separate tool costs.** Be clear about who pays for subscriptions, API usage and messaging.

## 6. Agree the scope in writing

Even a short message helps: what you will build, what is not included, how many rounds of changes they get, and when payment is due. It protects your friendship as well as your time. Then hand over simple documentation, and ask who else they know with the same problem.
